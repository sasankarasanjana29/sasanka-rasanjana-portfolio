VELA

A shopping app prototype from our team research thesis, made so Gen Z, Millennial and Gen X shoppers in Sri Lanka can search, decide and check out faster.

Overview

Why VELA

Mobile shoppers slow down at the same three points: searching, deciding and paying. VELA makes each of those faster and clearer.

The goal

Smooth the whole journey, from discovery to checkout, tracking and returns, with an interface that feels modern, minimal and trustworthy.

My role

In a team of three, I designed VELA: the flows, the visual system and the Figma prototype. The three of us ran the research together.

Research

From our team thesis at Uva Wellassa University (2025): three students, supervised by Ms. C.S.D. Ellepola, comparing how Gen X, Millennials and Gen Z in Sri Lanka use banking and shopping apps. Mixed methods, with ethics approval and consent from every participant.

~300survey responses: 113 Gen Z, 107 Millennials and 80 Gen X
9expert interviews with banking and e-commerce designers, 4 to 10 years in the field
60people in a moderated pilot on our Figma prototypes, 20 per generation

Survey

An online questionnaire for people aged 18 to 56 who already use banking or shopping apps, shared online and through campus and community networks.

Interviews

Semi-structured, 45 to 60 minutes each, online and in person, on generational pain points and the balance of security and ease.

Pilot

Moderated tasks on the prototypes, like signing in with a fingerprint or one-time code, paying, and checking out. Field notes only, used to fix the design.

Existing apps

A heuristic review of widely used Sri Lankan shopping apps against Nielsen Norman Group's ten usability heuristics.

01

Clean wins

Most people in every generation preferred a minimal, uncluttered design: 56% of Gen X, 61% of Millennials and 53% of Gen Z.

02

Different priorities

Gen X put a fast checkout first (44%). Millennials cared most about easy search (28%) and product reviews (23%).

03

So

Discovery and checkout had to be the strongest parts of the app. The pilot shaped labels, step count, tap targets, contrast and trust cues.

From finding to design

01

Checkout

  • Found Gen X put a fast checkout first (44%), and pilot sessions found cluttered checkout screens.
  • Designed Four predictable steps, cart, address, delivery and payment, with the total always on screen.
  • Pilot We cut checkout steps that added nothing, without weakening the safeguards.
02

Search

  • Found Millennials cared most about easy search (28%) and product reviews (23%).
  • Designed Recent and top searches, light filters that are easy to undo, and price and rating first on the product page.
  • Pilot Search is not in our pilot notes, so this part is still untested.
03

Trust

  • Found In the pilot, security steps were easy to miss or hard to understand.
  • Designed Plain-language security copy and familiar icons at checkout, and a failed payment that offers retry or another method.
  • Pilot This change came from the pilot itself.

What is not tested yet

The pilot shaped the design, but the finished screens have not been tested with users. The thesis plans that next: a quarter of each generation trying the prototype, then follow-up interviews.

Apps people use today

  • Busy home screens, hidden filters and checkout totals that appear late.

What shoppers wrote in reviews

  • Real store reviews: missing prices in search, slow refunds and orders marked delivered that never arrived.

The problem

01

Slow discovery

Search and filters are not obvious, so finding the right product takes too long.

02

Low confidence

Ratings, delivery and return details are hidden on product pages.

03

Checkout friction

Too many steps and totals that are unclear until the end.

04

Dead-end payments

A failed payment gives no clear way to recover.

05

Afterthought tracking

Order tracking and returns are hard to find.

06

Clutter

Busy screens directly cost speed and clarity.

Why it mattered

Shopping is a repeat habit, so small friction adds up. Every extra step or unclear total raises drop-offs and lowers trust.

Final screens

Sign in

  • Quick sign-in and a guest mode let people try the app first.
  • Language comes first, removing a barrier across generations.
  • Password guidance cuts failed sign-ups.

Discover and search

  • Clear categories and scannable product cards.
  • Recent and top searches save typing; filters are light and easy to undo.

Product page

  • Price, rating, variants, key details and the call to action, in that order.

Cart

  • Items can be removed with a swipe and brought back with Undo.
  • The total is always visible.

Pay, and recover when it fails

  • Predictable steps: cart, address, delivery, payment.
  • Success and failure both offer a next step: track the order, retry or change the method.
