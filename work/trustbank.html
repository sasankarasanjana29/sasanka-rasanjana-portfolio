Research

From our team thesis at Uva Wellassa University (2025): three students, supervised by Ms. C.S.D. Ellepola, comparing how Gen X, Millennials and Gen Z in Sri Lanka use banking and shopping apps. Mixed methods, with ethics approval and consent from every participant.

Survey
~300 people, aged 18 to 56
Interviews
9 designers, 45 to 60 min each
Pilot
60 people, 20 per generation
App review
Local banking apps vs 10 usability heuristics

Apps people use today

  • Dense home screens, small tap targets and features buried in menus.
  • Transfers with no clear review step, and receipts that are hard to find.

Safe, not exhausting

The experts asked for security that does not wear people out: biometrics by default, with a simple fallback for anyone who struggles with codes.

From finding to design

Sign-in50%of Gen X put face or fingerprint sign-in first.

DesignedFace ID by default, a code as the fallback.

PilotCodes and errors rewritten in plainer words.

Paying37%of Millennials put fast payment first.

DesignedOne review screen, then Face ID approves.

PilotSteps that repeated themselves removed.

Proof31%of Gen X ranked transaction history next.

DesignedA reference and a PDF receipt every time.

PilotLarger tap targets and stronger contrast.

What the pilot changed

Clearer one-time code and error messages, larger tap targets, stronger contrast, more visible trust cues, and fewer steps.

What is not tested yet

The pilot shaped the design, but the finished screens have not been tested with users. The thesis plans that next: a quarter of each generation trying the prototype, then follow-up interviews.

The problem

Banking is high stakes: unsure people slow down, make mistakes or stop trusting the app.

Basics are hard to findBalances and history take too many taps.
Transfers feel riskyNo easy review before confirming.
No real proofPeople fall back on screenshots.
Unclear alertsThey say what happened, not what to do.
Different generationsIt has to work at 20 and at 60.
